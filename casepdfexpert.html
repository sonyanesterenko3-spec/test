04 · 4 problems we found

The decision to pay gets made
before the user feels the value

Synthesis boiled everything down to four concrete funnel problems — each backed by client data or secondary research.

P1 · Onboarding asks for profession

The screen asks "Which profession is closest to you?" But profession is a poor predictor of behavior: an IT person learning French doesn't identify with any option — they tap randomly or hit "other." Profession-based personalization already failed in A/B.

P2 · Aha comes too late

Aha — the document viewer, opening the first file — happens AFTER the paywall, already on trial. So the decision to pay gets made before the user has felt any value, and 70% of subscriptions are decided right there in onboarding.

P3 · Generic paywall

The paywall sells with generic features ("The Only PDF Tool You'll Ever Need"), not the language of the user's specific task. A paywall personalized by profession already failed in A/B — segmentation needs a different axis.

P4 · Fill&sign friction

Fill&sign is the job of the highest-paying segment (Sales, Legal). But the signature has to be found deep inside the app — friction on the way to the first signed document, which is that job's aha.

06 · Hypotheses

Five hypotheses —
one flow that scales across every job

Each hypothesis is framed as "If... then... because..." and maps directly to one of the four problems above. I personally led two of them — the intent screen and the personalized paywall — including the A/B mockup variants.

H1 · Intent instead of profession my hypothesis

If we replace the profession picker with an intent screen — "What brought you to PDF Expert?" (multi-select jobs + context) — the answer will be more relevant and CR to trial will grow, because profession doesn't equal intent, but a job does.

H2 · First file as an onboarding step

If we add an "open or scan your first file" step right into onboarding, aha (the document viewer) will happen before the paywall instead of after — the user will feel the value before the moment they decide to pay.

H3 · Paywall in the job's language my hypothesis

If the paywall's copy and social proof adapt to the job the user just picked, the paywall will speak the language of their task instead of generic features — and CR to trial will grow, most visibly in Business & Sales.

N1 · Signature inside onboarding

If we let users create and save a signature right inside onboarding (draw / photo / upload) for the fill&sign job, we remove friction for the highest-paying segment on the way to their first signed document.

N2 · Entry point for existing users

Low-effort bonus: an "Already have an account?" button on screen 1 — it targets the single biggest drop-off in the whole funnel, with no rework needed for the rest of the flow.

07 · Redesign

Intent, first file,
paywall in the job's language

The new flow: intent screen (job) → first file/scan → personalized paywall → an app configured for that job. I designed the intent screen, the first-document step, and the paywall variants tailored to the user's specific task.

Starting point — today's onboarding: a generic toolset and a generic paywall, with no connection to the user's task

H1 + H3 · The "Where do we start?" intent screen and a paywall adapted to the chosen job (fill&sign)

H2 · First file as an onboarding step

"Add your first document" → "Your document is ready" — the user adds or scans a file right inside onboarding, and aha happens before they ever see the price.

Setup → Aha for fill&sign: add a document and the app is already set up to sign it — no wondering what to do next

"Doesn't reach the paywall" and "reaches it but doesn't see the value" are two different problems with two different fixes: one is about the screen's language, the other is about when value actually shows up.

08 · Outcomes

What the defense showed —
and why I'm carrying it forward

We defended the work in front of the Growth Design course mentors, and the feedback confirmed the direction: moving from demographic personalization (profession) to intent-based personalization (job) reads as the logical next step for a product that had already watched its own blunt personalization fail in A/B.

The biggest lesson for me as a designer — you can't put the aha moment after the paywall and hope people pay blind. If 70% of decisions are made during onboarding, then onboarding — not the product itself — is the point where we either prove value or lose the user for good.
